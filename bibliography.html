This page lists the five scholarly sources used in the tutorial, numbered in the order they first appear on the site. Each citation link on the other pages jumps to the matching entry here.

For every source, I give the full citation, a DOI link, a short synopsis, a reliability rating, and an explanation of why it is useful for this tutorial. All five were published in 2025 or 2026, in peer-reviewed journals or conference proceedings.

I also note which version of each paper I read. Four of the five are open access, and I read the fifth through my university library.

Reliability scale used here: High means peer-reviewed in a major venue, with methods and results I checked in the full text of the paper. Four of the five sources are open access. Xu et al. [2] requires library access.

[1] A decade of action quality assessment: Largest systematic survey of trends, challenges, and future directions

Yin, H., Parmar, P., Xu, D., Zhang, Y., Zheng, T., & Fu, W. (2026). A decade of action quality assessment: Largest systematic survey of trends, challenges, and future directions. International Journal of Computer Vision, 134(2), Article 73. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-025-02672-4

Synopsis: A systematic review of more than 200 AQA papers. It defines the task, explains the standard metrics and datasets, groups recent work into seven trends, and lists open challenges such as the lack of a gold standard, small datasets, and black-box models.

Reliability: High The International Journal of Computer Vision is a long-running, peer-reviewed Springer journal and one of the main journals in the field. Co-author Paritosh Parmar has published widely on AQA. I read the open-access arXiv preprint (v1, February 2025), and some counts in the published version may differ slightly.

Why it is useful: It gives the tutorial its vocabulary and its frame for the field. The definitions in Section 4 and most of the challenges in Section 6 come from this survey.

[2] Human-centric fine-grained action quality assessment

Xu, J., Yin, S., & Peng, Y. (2025). Human-centric fine-grained action quality assessment. IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, 47(8), 6242–6255. https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3556935

Synopsis: Proposes Uni-FineParser, which focuses on the athlete in each frame and parses each dive into steps over time before scoring it. It beats earlier methods on three benchmarks, and its ablations show that the temporal parsing step matters most.

Reliability: High IEEE TPAMI is one of the most selective peer-reviewed journals in computer vision. I read the full published version through CSU East Bay's IEEE Xplore access. The paper is paywalled, but the code is public, and an earlier version (FineParser, CVPR 2024) is open access.

Why it is useful: It is the clearest statement of why fine-grained, phase-aware parsing is needed, and its focus on separating the athlete from the background maps directly onto separating a runner from a busy scene.

[3] I3D-AE-LSTM: A 2-stream autoencoder for action quality assessment using a newly created cricket batsman video dataset

Moodley, T., & van der Haar, D. T. (2025). I3D-AE-LSTM: A 2-stream autoencoder for action quality assessment using a newly created cricket batsman video dataset. In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV) (pp. 5470–5478). https://doi.org/10.1109/WACV61041.2025.00534

Synopsis: Introduces a dataset of 8,540 cricket strokes split into phases and scored for five body regions. A two-stream model (I3D frame features plus an LSTM autoencoder over keypoints) reaches an average Spearman correlation of 0.84, though it overestimates rare poor strokes.

Reliability: High WACV is a peer-reviewed IEEE/CVF conference. I read the full open-access version from the Computer Vision Foundation, and the dataset and code are publicly available. The evaluation covers a single sport and a single dataset, so the results should be read within that scope.

Why it is useful: It is the best example in this tutorial of phase-structured, body-region scoring, and its data imbalance problem is an honest look at a real limitation.

[4] FineCausal: A causal-based framework for interpretable fine-grained action quality assessment

Han, R., Zhou, K., Atapour-Abarghouei, A., Liang, X., & Shum, H. P. H. (2025). FineCausal: A causal-based framework for interpretable fine-grained action quality assessment. In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition Workshops (CVPRW) (pp. 6064–6073). https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPRW67362.2025.00599

Synopsis: Separates athlete features from background and uses masked self-attention to model how each stage of a dive affects the next. It slightly beats the previous best method on FineDiving-HM (ρ = 0.9447) while keeping stage segmentation highly accurate, but it needs expert knowledge and costly labels.

Reliability: High This is a peer-reviewed paper from the CVsports workshop at CVPR, the field's largest conference. Workshop papers are usually reviewed less strictly than main-conference papers. I read the full open-access version, and the code is public.

Why it is useful: It shows clearly how temporal attention can model the chain of phases, and it is the main source here for segmentation accuracy and for interpretability.

[5] UIL-AQA: Uncertainty-aware clip-level interpretable action quality assessment

Dong, X., Liu, X., Li, W., Adeyemi-Ejeye, A., & Gilbert, A. (2025). UIL-AQA: Uncertainty-aware clip-level interpretable action quality assessment. International Journal of Computer Vision. Published online December 28, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-025-02638-6

Synopsis: Scores long gymnastics, skating, and artistic-swimming routines with a transformer decoder that gives every clip its own difficulty and quality score. It fixes a "temporal skipping" problem with an extra attention loss and adds noise during training to account for disagreement between judges.

Reliability: High The International Journal of Computer Vision is a leading peer-reviewed Springer journal. This article is open access, so I read the full text, and the code is public. It extends the authors' earlier BMVC 2024 paper.

Why it is useful: It is the only source here that handles long videos and models disagreement between judges, and running videos are long and labels for running form are subjective.

All five sources were published in 2025 or 2026. No older background sources were added.
